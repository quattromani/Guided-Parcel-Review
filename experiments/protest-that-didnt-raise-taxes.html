Case Study

The Protest That Didn't Raise Taxes

In 2025, this property owner protested a valuation increase before the Board of Equalization. The protest was denied. Surprisingly, the property's taxes still went down.

Assessment changes and tax changes are not always the same thing.

2. What Actually Happened?

The protest was unsuccessful. The tax bill still went down.

Even though the protest was unsuccessful, taxes declined. This happened because taxes are influenced by more than a property's individual assessment.

2025 Net Taxes $1,410.22

Assessment Change + Countywide Value Growth + Levy Compression + Budget Growth = Final Tax Bill

3. Why Did Taxes Go Down?

The levy compressed faster than this property's value increased

When many properties increase together, levy rates often compress. A property can receive a higher valuation and still experience little change, or even a decrease, in taxes.

Property Value Change +4.75%
Gross Levy Movement -10.03%
Net Effective Tax Rate 0.695% → 0.640%

Net tax divided by assessed value moved down about 8.0%.

Levy Compression Shorthand

Adjusted rate = current rate × ((1 + budget growth) ÷ (1 + countywide growth))

If the countywide value base grows faster than budgets, the rate needed to fund the same obligations can move lower.

6. The Bigger Lesson

What We Learned

  • Protest denied
  • Assessment increased
  • Taxes still decreased
  • Assessment increased substantially again
  • Taxes likely increase this year
  • Tax increase is expected to be much smaller than the valuation increase alone would suggest

The valuation notice tells us how a property moved. The tax bill tells us what that movement ultimately cost. Those are related, but they are not the same thing.
