Case Study

Assessment Up. Protest Denied. Taxes Down.

In 2025, a property owner appeared before the Board of Equalization to protest a valuation increase. The Board left the value unchanged. The following year, the owner's taxes went down anyway.

This case study follows that property through two assessment cycles and shows a repeatable way to think about valuation changes and tax impact.

Assessment changes and tax changes are not always the same thing.

3. How Should We Think About This?

Start with movement, not just value

The useful question is how this property moved compared with the larger tax base and the revenue needed to fund local budgets.

Property Movement

How much did this property move?

+4.75%

The parcel's assessed value moved from $210,510 to $220,510.

County Movement

How much did everyone else move?

Countywide growth

When many properties increase together, the tax base expands.

Budget Movement

How much additional revenue was needed?

Budget growth

Budgets determine how much tax pressure must be collected.

Property Movement vs County Movement + Budget Growth = Tax Pressure

If the broader tax base moves too, levy rates can compress. That is the bridge between assessment change and tax change.

5. What Might Happen Next?

Estimated 2026 tax impact

This year's question may sound like last year's: what is this value change likely to do to my taxes?

Part A

What If Tax Rates Never Changed?

This is often the first calculation taxpayers make. If the 2025 net effective tax rate stayed the same, the 2026 value would produce a larger increase before any levy compression.

Baseline only — not the likely outcome.

Same-rate taxes $1,822.75
Annual change +$412.53
Monthly equivalent +$34.38

Part B

Why Is That Probably Not The Final Answer?

Most properties moved too. The tax base expanded. If countywide value growth outpaces budget growth, levy rates often compress.

2025 net ETR 0.640%
Budget growth 3.00%
Countywide value growth 9.57%
Estimated adjusted rate 0.601%
Current rate × (1 + budget growth) ÷ (1 + countywide growth) = adjusted rate

Part C

What Does That Mean For This Property?

After the baseline is adjusted for county movement and budget movement, the likely outcome is much smaller than the value increase alone might suggest.

Most taxpayers begin with a valuation notice.

Most elected officials begin with a budget.

The tax bill is produced somewhere in the middle.

Calculation Detail

How The Estimate Was Built

Use the same framework: find current tax pressure, adjust for county movement and budget movement, then apply it to the new value.

Step 1

Find the current tax pressure

2025 net tax ÷ 2025 value $1,410.22 ÷ $220,510 = 0.640%
Step 2

Adjust for levy compression

Current rate × (1 + budget growth) ÷ (1 + countywide growth) 0.640% × (1.03 ÷ 1.0957) = 0.601%
Step 3

Apply the adjusted rate

2026 value × adjusted rate $285,015 × 0.601% = $1,713.45
Estimated taxes $1,713.45
Annual change +$303.23
Monthly equivalent +$25.27

This is not a tax bill prediction. It is an estimate based on historical levy behavior, countywide valuation growth, and reasonable budget assumptions.

6. The Bigger Lesson

What We Learned

What Happened

  • Assessment increased
  • Protest denied
  • Taxes decreased

The Framework

  • Start with property movement
  • Compare it to county movement
  • Account for budget growth
  • Estimate tax pressure

The valuation notice tells us how a property moved. The tax bill tells us what that movement ultimately cost.
