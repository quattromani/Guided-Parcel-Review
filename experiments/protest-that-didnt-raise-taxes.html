Case Study

Assessment Up. Protest Denied. Taxes Down.

In 2025, a property owner appeared before the Board of Equalization to protest a valuation increase. The Board left the value unchanged. The following year, the owner's taxes went down anyway.

This case study follows that property through two assessment cycles and shows a repeatable way to think about valuation changes and tax impact.

Assessment changes and tax changes are not always the same thing.

3. The Missing Rule

Why can this happen?

Taxes are not based on your value alone. Taxes are based on how your value moved relative to everything around you.

15-Second Explanation

A property tax bill is not determined by your value alone.

It is determined by how your property moved, how other properties moved, and how much local governments decide to collect.

That is why assessments and taxes can move in different directions.
Myth Assessment up means taxes up.
Reality Assessment up changes your share of the tax base.
Actual Drivers Property movement, countywide movement, and budget growth.

The Missing Piece: Taxes Follow Relative Movement

Why does relative movement matter? Because taxes are about share. A valuation notice tells you how your property moved. A tax bill tells you how the whole system moved.

Most homeowners would look at this notice and expect higher taxes.

That outcome only makes sense if something bigger than this property was happening at the same time.

4. What Levy Compression Means

The rate can fall when the base grows faster than the budget

What Is Levy Compression?

Think of a group splitting a bill. If the bill grows a little but the group gets much larger, each person's share can shrink.

When many properties rise together, the tax rate does not always need to rise with them. If the tax base grows faster than the budget, tax rates can compress downward. Values rise, rates fall, and the tax bill may stay flat or even decrease.

8. The Bigger Lesson

The Question That Changes Everything

A higher assessment can produce higher taxes, lower taxes, or almost no change. The answer depends on how this property moved compared with everything else and how much local governments decide to spend.

Use this on your next notice

Most homeowners ask: What is my new value?

A better question is: How did my property move compared with everyone else?
