# Nebraska valuation protests, notices, and budget hearings change in 2027

If you plan to challenge your property's assessed value in 2027, prepare your supporting documents before you file. Nebraska's LB 803 strengthens the documentation requirement and expressly makes **missing required documentation grounds for dismissal**.

The law also changes valuation notices and moves the joint public budget hearing from September to July. These provisions become operative January 1, 2027. [Enacted LB 803, sections 14–18 and 25](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB803.pdf)

## A valuation protest needs reasons and documentation

The earlier law required reasons for the requested change, the requested valuation, and a description of the property. LB 803 adds an express requirement for documentation sufficient for the county board of equalization to determine a different valuation. It also adds that documentation requirement to the grounds for dismissal. [LB 803, section 15](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB803.pdf)

**Your protest must include support for the value you are requesting.** Do not assume you can file an unsupported objection and bring all your evidence later.

The amendment does not require comparable sales in every case. It requires documentation sufficient to support a different valuation. Explain what your documents show and how they support your requested value.

*Dismissal and denial are different.* A protest can be dismissed for failing to meet the statutory filing requirements. A protest that meets those requirements can still be denied if the board considers the evidence and does not find grounds to change the value.

Counties may try to contact taxpayers about incomplete filings, but the law does not guarantee an opportunity to complete them later. [Valuation-protest requirements](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-1502)

## June 30 remains the real-property valuation protest deadline

File your annual real-property valuation protest with the county clerk by **June 30**, including your requested value, reasons, supporting documentation, and property description. The county board of equalization decides the protest.

The new July budget hearing does not extend that deadline. Speaking at a budget hearing does not substitute for filing a valuation protest. [Section 77-1502](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-1502)

## Valuation-change notices will show tax comparisons

When the assessed value of taxable real estate changes, the county assessor must mail the owner a notice by June 1. Beginning in 2027, that notice will show the previous and current valuations alongside tax information for the city, county, and school district.

The current-year comparison applies **last year's tax rates to this year's assessed value**. It shows what those governments would levy if their rates stayed the same.

Those figures exclude homestead exemptions and property-tax credits. **They are not your final tax bill.** The mailing requirement applies to properties with changed valuations; it does not mean every owner will receive a notice. [Valuation-notice requirements](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-1315)

## The joint public budget hearing moves to July

Beginning in 2027, the joint public hearing moves from September to **July 1 through July 14, after 6 p.m.** Counties, cities, and school districts must participate. A government spanning multiple counties participates in the county containing its principal headquarters.

Representatives will explain the budget process, the previous year's budget and property-tax request, and preliminary factors affecting the current budget. The county assessor and at least one voting member of each participating governing body must attend. Members of the public must be allowed a reasonable amount of time to speak.

This hearing concerns budgets and tax requests. It does not decide individual valuation protests or adopt final budgets. [Joint public hearing requirements](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-1629)

The July process replaces the September joint-hearing system tied to specified tax-request increases. Other required budget and tax-request hearings remain. [LB 803, sections 17–18 and 29](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/FloorDocs/109/PDF/Slip/LB803.pdf)

## Check the schedule and prepare early

Owners receiving valuation-change notices will also be sent a state postcard directing them to the county website for hearing information. Confirm meeting details with the government holding the hearing, because schedules can change. [Notice and postcard requirements](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=77-1315)

If you question your valuation, review your property information and assemble documentation supporting the value you believe is appropriate. Contact the county clerk for protest filing information.

If your concern is government spending or the amount requested in property taxes, follow the budget hearings for the county, city, or school district responsible. Those discussions offer a separate opportunity to ask questions and be heard.
