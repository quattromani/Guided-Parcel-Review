# GES Typography

Typography is the primary hierarchy tool. Color should support, not carry, hierarchy.

## Text Roles

- Publication / cover title: `ges-publication-title`, `article-hero .hero-title`
- Narrative section title: `ges-narrative-title`, article section `h2`
- Component heading: `ges-component-heading`, component `h3`
- Kicker / eyebrow: `guided-kicker`, `section-kicker`, `ges-kicker`
- Companion line: `ges-section-companion`
- Body copy: `prose`, `ges-prose`
- Caption: `ges-caption`, `figcaption`
- Metadata: `ges-meta`, `article-entry-meta`
- Utility text: `ges-utility-text`
- Citation/source text: `ges-citation-text`, `article-source-note`

## Cover Versus Reading Mode

The publication title is not always the largest visual text. The cover title introduces the guide. Narrative section headings pull the reader into the reading experience.

Use a restrained hero `h1` and let the first major `h2` carry more reading-mode weight when the article needs that transition.

## Editorial Rhythm

A large heading should usually be followed by one of:

- body copy
- companion line
- diagram
- process strip
- margin insight

Avoid heading-to-heading-to-heading sequences.

After dense components, give the eye quiet prose or whitespace. Do not place two high-attention components back to back unless the relationship is explicit.

## Kicker Rules

Kickers orient the reader in the journey. They should name the stage of thought, not merely label the topic.

Good:

- `Finding Good Comparables`
- `Speaking to the Board`
- `Gathering Your Materials`

Weak:

- `Comparables`
- `At the Hearing`
- `Resources`

## Body Copy

Body copy should remain narrow enough for sustained reading. Use the reading width unless a component benefits from a wider measure.

Do not center body text.
