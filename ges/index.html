Guided Editorial System 1.0

A civic editorial design system for guides, explainers, tools, and public field notes.

GES reduces cognitive friction without reducing intellectual depth. Its components teach, orient, reassure, clarify, verify, or help the reader act.

Principles

Section Rhythm

First section stays uncreased

The first story chapter starts the reading path. It does not need a divider because nothing substantive comes before it.

Creases are section boundaries, not content blocks.

Specialized Components

Components that teach or help readers act

  1. Find

    2-3 good comparable properties.

  2. Compare

    The property records.

  3. Document

    The specific difference.

  4. Request

    One clear correction.

  1. What appears incorrect?

    Name the record fact or value issue as plainly as possible.

  2. What evidence supports that?

    Attach the photo, measurement, record, sale, or comparable that helps verify it.

  3. What correction are you requesting?

    Ask for the specific record or value adjustment you want considered.

Component Guidance

When to use patterns

Article Cover

Purpose: introduce the guide calmly. Use for standalone articles. Do not use inside app task surfaces. Accessibility: one H1; media has alt/caption. Print: preserve title and metadata.

article-hero

Margin Insight

Purpose: orient skimmers with a short cue. Use sparingly, usually 6-14 words. Adjacent story sections receive the full-width paper-fold crease separately; first story sections stay uncreased.

ges-margin-insight

Section Crease

Purpose: divide adjacent story sections after prior article content. It is generated by the section rhythm rule, not authored with an HR or route helper.

.tax-story-chapter + .tax-story-chapter

Process Strip

Purpose: show workflow. Use for ordered steps. Do not use for independent checks. Accessibility: ordered list. Print: avoid breaking inside.

process-strip

Decision Tree

Purpose: frame a practical question. Use when a binary branch clarifies the reader's next thought. Do not use for full legal procedure. Accessibility: text labels carry meaning.

decision-panel

Evidence Matrix

Purpose: translate observation into requested correction. Do not repeat labels in every row. Mobile: rows stack in source order. Print: keep rows together.

evidence-matrix

Practical Note

Purpose: clarify, caution, calibrate, or explain procedure. Icons must be semantic. Do not use generic attention styling for routine prose.

ges-practical-note

Body Icon Circle

Purpose: give small circular body icons a slight paper lift. Use for editorial markers in prose components. Do not apply to buttons, navigation, footer utility icons, form controls, chart markers, or large illustrations.

ges-body-icon-circle

Authority Citation

Purpose: support trust without interrupting reading. Use muted treatment. Do not put routine citations in loud cards.

article-source-note

Resource Panel

Purpose: link official forms, maps, and records. Use meaningful link text and visible print URLs. Do not use for loose extra reading.

resource-section

Resources Block

Purpose: consolidate authorities after the article and related-reading offer. Include relied-upon sources only; avoid footer bibliographies.

ges-resources-block

Continuation Module

Purpose: preserve the reading journey before sources. Use even with one linked next step. Do not bury it under citations.

continuation-module

Finding The Disconnect

Why do sincere protests fail?

Sincerity explains concern. Evidence supports correction.

Usually not because the homeowner is careless. More often, the protest asks the Board to solve the wrong problem.

A hearing cannot inspect a feeling. It needs something it can compare, measure, confirm, or correct.
